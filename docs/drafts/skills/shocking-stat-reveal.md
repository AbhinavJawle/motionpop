## Intent
Make one number feel heavy and unforgettable. This is the moment that defines the scene.

## Shot sequence (timing as % of duration)
1. **0–10%**: Muted context frame or darkness. Build anticipation. Nothing moves yet.
2. **10–30%**: Slow push-in toward the centre (ease-in-out). Camera scale 1.0 → 1.08.
3. **30–55%**: Number counts up fast, decelerating over the last 20% (feels heavy, like it's landing).
4. **55%**: Hard stop + micro camera shake (2 frames, 1px) + impact SFX.
5. **55–85%**: HOLD. Nothing else moves. Let the viewer read and absorb.
6. **85–100%**: Context line fades in at 60% opacity below the number.

## Camera
- Single slow push-in (scale 1.0 → 1.08 over 10–55%)
- No rotation
- Micro shake at the stop (2 frames)

## Emphasis
- Accent colour ONLY on the final number
- Everything else stays muted (use muted palette role)
- No competing visual elements during the hold

## Sound cues
- Optional: rising tension sound under the count-up (10–50%)
- Impact sound at the hard stop (55%)
- Silence during the hold

## Rules
- One number per frame. Never show multiple competing numbers.
- Never cut away or introduce new elements before the hold completes.
- No competing motion during the hold period.
- The hold must be at least 30% of total duration.

## Anti-patterns
- ❌ Bouncy or elastic easing on the number (makes it feel light)
- ❌ Multiple accent colours (dilutes emphasis)
- ❌ Text appearing during the count-up (distracts from the number)
- ❌ Continuous camera movement during hold (breaks the moment)

## Voice sync (if voiceover present)
- Align the hard stop (55%) with the stressed word in the voice
- Example: "...grew to **one trillion**" — the stop happens on "trillion"
